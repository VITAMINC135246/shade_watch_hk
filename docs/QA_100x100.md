## Method checks

Eight synthetic tests pass, covering flat terrain, an isolated 20 m building's westward 45° shadow length under eastern sun, a slope, a blocker across a tile boundary, NoData propagation, azimuth bins, date and time-zone handling, and the one-dimensional horizon hull against brute-force slopes. Rays traverse **toward** the Sun; shadows extend oppositely. The solar centre stays above the astronomical horizon for all 65 MP4 frames, so no nighttime frame is counted as building shade.

At 14:30 HKT, 400 spatially distributed real-DSM points were compared with an independently coded 0.5 m direct-ray reference. The resampled-rotation baseline had a median absolute horizon difference of 0.36° and a 95th percentile of 21.99°. The pixel-centred 0.25 m ray scan used for the deliverables had a median of 0.00° and 95th percentile of 2.80° (maximum 8.62°). The baseline and refined 14:30 binary rasters differed at 1,789 of 40,000 valid pixels (4.47%). The [method notes](METHOD.md) explain this numerical departure from whole-raster rotation. Agreement between these DSM-based implementations is **not** evidence of real-world accuracy.

These comparisons use the supplied DSM only. Zero class changes with a shorter ray at these three times does not prove distant obstructions are absent. The 1 km coverage and low-sun flags remain active. In the 65-frame run, incomplete sunward DSM support produced invalid/uncertain pixels in 17 morning frames (maximum 7,676 pixels). Distant-obstruction sensitivity produced invalid/uncertain pixels in four late-afternoon frames (maximum 3,131 pixels). The conservative low-sun threshold was 18.51° using the highest buffered DSM cell (402.60 m HKPD), lowest output cell (67.82 m HKPD), and 1 km distance. Unseen terrain outside the buffer remains an unresolved limitation even above that threshold.

## Output and visual checks

The calculated astronomical daylight spans **07:03:33–17:53:13 HKT**. The MP4 has 65 distinct computed frames at 10-minute clock times from 07:10 to 17:50 HKT; each frame's UTC and local timestamp, solar geometry, source path, class counts, and quality counts are in the inventory. This is not the paper's separate 06:00–18:00 analysis window.

All 65 GeoTIFFs were reopened. They are EPSG:2326, 200 × 200, share the exact expected bounds and timestamps, and contain only 0, 1, or explicit NoData 255. Every corresponding PNG has identical valid labels and zero alpha for invalid pixels. Every quality raster contains only documented codes 0–4. The MP4 was decoded and contains exactly 65 frames at 960 × 800, 4 fps, H.264; representative decoded frames match their source presentation PNGs within expected lossy compression. The 14:30 JPG and representative MP4 frames were visually inspected for fixed extent, orientation, legend, time, and scale. The local timeline viewer was checked at 07:10 and 14:30, including slider, playback, and EPSG:2326 pointer coordinates.

## Independent photo

The supplied DJI image was visually inspected and has a nadir gimbal-pitch field (−90°). Its XMP `UTCAtExposure` is **2026-01-07 06:33:02.040750 UTC**, or **14:33:02.040750 HKT**; its timezone-free EXIF clock reads 14:32:59, about three seconds earlier. EXIF GPS is near the configured centre. A separate real-DSM result was calculated at the XMP exposure instant in `outputs/validation_capture/` (solar azimuth 216.15°, apparent elevation 35.88°; LUT bin 215°). The photo's surveyed ground footprint, camera calibration/control points, independent pixel labels, and weather/cloud record are not provided. The source configuration still says validation “Not available” despite the image's presence; it was left unchanged. The photo cannot support a defensible registered pixel-by-pixel accuracy score. Tree canopy tops and any scene change since the 2020 DSM add interpretation uncertainty.
